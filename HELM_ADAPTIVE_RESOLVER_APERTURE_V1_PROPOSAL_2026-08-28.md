# HELM Adaptive Resolver Aperture v1 — Proposal and Falsification Design

**Status:** PROPOSAL ONLY — NOT IMPLEMENTED

## 1. Executive summary

T0 is a real operational loss. HELM used 205 logical provider calls against 153 for the competent broad-context arm, spent approximately 9.3% more on the provider, and carried approximately 39.3% more mean composite burden. The critical-event difference was not statistically significant. The perfect HELM terminal score was materially aided by the exposed control schedule and is not scientific evidence of comparative superiority.

T0 nevertheless supports a narrower engineering hypothesis: HELM may be using the same expensive reasoning topology in situations whose governed structure permits deterministic reuse, a narrow projection, or one wide but exactly bounded Resolver call. This proposal calls that hypothesis **Adaptive Resolver Aperture v1**. It is an ephemeral execution-selection policy, not a new canonical object or authority.

The controller would select only among existing execution behaviors. Governed structure—not a model—would determine eligibility. Authority, Gate requirements, invalidation, and minimum-sufficient evidence remain unchanged. If deterministic evidence cannot establish a cheaper path safely, execution fails closed to the existing full governed examination path.

T0 does not show that this proposal can cross the economic baseline. Post-hoc accounting indicates that the +52 logical-call gap is theoretically removable in call count, principally by replacing three fact-level calls with one Gate-declared wide call in the 30 coupled tasks. That change is not a demonstrated cost saving: the T0 broad-context analog was more expensive at the provider than the three sparse calls. The clearest deterministic-reuse opportunity is only about six calls and approximately $0.0071 of provider spend. Most composite excess came from human-examination accounting that Resolver steering cannot lawfully remove unless those examinations are independently shown to be unauthorized or redundant.

Therefore T1 must be capable of killing the hypothesis. It must use a new sealed corpus, compare frozen current HELM, adaptive HELM, and a competent broad-context baseline contemporaneously, preserve a hidden benchmark-authority schedule, and enforce zero-tolerance governance gates before economics are considered.

## 6. Avoidable versus unavoidable burden

**POST-HOC ENGINEERING ANALYSIS — NOT VALIDATION**

### Logical calls

The 30 coupled tasks used 90 H calls. One Gate-bound wide call per task would use 30, removing 60 calls and moving the T0-shaped H total from 205 to 145. That is enough to remove the observed +52-call gap in count. I1 additionally exposes a theoretical maximum of six repeat calls if every unchanged repeat is deterministically intercepted before Probe. These are upper-bound opportunities, not measured savings.

### Provider spend

Logical-call reduction does not imply token-cost reduction. In coupled T0, the one-call broad analog cost $0.136169 versus $0.114391 for H's three sparse calls. Widening would likely trade call count and latency exposure for a larger prompt.

I1's 12 calls cost $0.014170. Treating six as removable repeats gives a rough upper bound of approximately $0.007085 avoided provider spend. That is only about 33% of the $0.021422 total provider gap. T0 therefore does **not** show that Resolver steering alone can cross the provider baseline.

### Composite burden

The aggregate mean-composite gap is approximately $0.039160 per task, or about $5.991 across 153 tasks. The clearly steerable I1 provider opportunity is approximately $0.0071 total—about $0.000046 per task. The rest is dominated by human-examination contributions and non-critical harm scoring. Resolver selection has no authority to remove a required human Gate. T0 therefore provides no defensible basis for claiming the proposed controller can achieve the 28.2% composite reduction target.

### Burden that must remain

- human authorization required by an active Gate;
- examination after material invalidation, conflict, authority change, or lifecycle crossing;
- semantic discovery where no governed relationship exists;
- first-time governed rejection/acceptance and graduation costs;
- complete jointly sufficient evidence when facts are genuinely coupled;
- provenance, version, lineage, and fail-closed validation overhead;
- provider failure retention and symmetric retry accounting.

## 7. Candidate architecture classification

**Provisional classification: existing-contract enforcement plus implementation hardening.**

Frozen HELM already separates Resolver proposals from Governor authorization, permits deterministic and model-backed Resolvers, supports an ephemeral bounded Payload, binds Gate `required_evidence`, and supports version/lineage/validity-based reuse. Selecting among those existing paths does not require a new canonical object if the selection decision remains ephemeral and reproducible.

This classification fails if implementation needs a new persisted aperture authority, lets a model decide whether governance applies, or adds undeclared coupling/relationship semantics to the Domain Pack after observing tasks.

## 8. Existing HELM contracts reused

- Human Anchor and Path establish mission and current target.
- Gate identity, state, version, and `required_evidence` establish the authorized decision surface.
- Canonical Fact identity, version, authority, validity, and lineage establish reusable evidence.
- Existing dependency/coupling expression distinguishes sequential from jointly required evidence.
- Decision, MutationEvent, and LineageRecord support prior-result reuse and invalidation.
- ResolverProposal remains non-authoritative.
- Authority Governor and human Gates retain all write authority.
- Probe remains CandidateForExamination discovery only.
- Existing bounded Payload projection supplies minimum-sufficient evidence.

No new `Aperture`, `Mode`, or `ControllerDecision` canonical primitive is proposed. An execution receipt may record which existing path was used, but it must be derived telemetry/provenance rather than new authority.

## 9. Proposed execution modes

### 9.1 Deterministic reuse/bypass

Eligibility requires an exact governed identity or already-graduated relationship, valid lineage and versions, unexpired `valid_while`, no matching `invalidated_by` condition, and unchanged Gate/effect scope. The result is reused without Probe, model call, or repeat human examination.

### 9.2 Sparse aperture

Use the current minimum-sufficient projection when dependencies are known and decomposable or when the active Gate's evidence set is small relative to the relevant governed surface. Resolver sees only the version-bound evidence required for the next move.

### 9.3 Wide governed aperture

If the current Gate already declares a jointly required evidence set and all required evidence is valid and present, serialize that exact set once for one Resolver execution. Do not expose the Slice, Domain Pack, or Canonical state beyond that set. Missing, stale, conflicting, or unauthorized evidence fails closed.

### 9.4 Full governed examination

Preserve the current protected path for conflict, invalidation, authority uncertainty, lifecycle transition, consequential rejection, incompatible authoritative evidence, or any human authorization requirement. Aperture selection may change model topology; it cannot remove examination or authorization.

### 9.5 Probe/discovery

Use Probe only where deterministic governed structure cannot express the potentially material relationship. Probe may nominate CandidateForExamination. It may not choose the aperture, declare reuse, establish coupling, authorize, or mutate.

## 11. Authority-boundary analysis

The controller chooses execution topology, never authority. It cannot close a Gate, change Anchor or Path, accept evidence, suppress after invalidation, create a dependency, or approve external effect. All steering inputs must already be governed or deterministic runtime facts.

Compute, latency, or predicted cost may not downgrade full examination to reuse or bypass. They may select between a single joint Resolver call and a sequential Resolver sequence only when both consume the same authorized minimum-sufficient evidence and produce the same non-authoritative proposal contract.

## 12. Minimum-sufficient-surface analysis

Minimum sufficient does not mean smallest possible token count. It means the least governed evidence that can safely adjudicate the active Gate. A decomposable Gate may expose one condition at a time. A coupled Gate may require 100 facts simultaneously. Both are sparse relative to arbitrary full-state exposure if the Gate declares the boundary.

Selection must validate: current Gate/version; exact required IDs; fact validity and authority; complete retrieval; no unresolved conflict; and Payload hash. If any check fails, the Resolver receives no partial adjudication surface.

## 13. Dense-coupling analysis

Prior coupled evidence already established that Gate `required_evidence` can express the joint surface without a new primitive. Therefore one wide call over the exact jointly sufficient set is consistent with frozen HELM.

T0 shows why the hypothesis must be falsified rather than assumed. Three sparse calls cost less than the one-call broad analog, even though they increased call count. T1 must measure tokens, provider failures, latency, and correctness separately. It must permit the result that wide aperture reduces calls but worsens cost or reliability.

## 14. Deterministic-reuse analysis

Reuse is valid only when the prior governed result's identity, effect scope, Gate version, evidence lineage, validity conditions, authority conditions, lifecycle coordinates, and invalidators still match. It must be a read-only deterministic lookup.

The strongest safety counterexample is a paraphrased or re-ingested restriction that looks equivalent to a previously rejected candidate but contains a changed authority, effective date, or negation. If generalized reuse suppresses it, HELM becomes consequentially blind. Therefore v1 performs no fuzzy, embedding, lexical, or model equivalence at suppression time. Identity misses remain visible and flow to Probe/examination.

## 15. Probe/discovery boundary

Probe remains necessary for unknown semantic relationships. Aperture selection cannot use Probe's nomination as truth. The sequence remains:

`Latent fact → Probe nomination → CandidateForExamination → governed examination → authorized structure/decision → deterministic future handling`.

Probe false nominations remain a human-attention and provider-cost burden. v1 does not add rejection memory or semantic identity.

## 16. Human-attention analysis

T0 composite loss is dominated by human-examination accounting in several same-correctness categories. This is not automatically waste. T1 must distinguish:

1. **required authority attention**, which cannot be removed;
2. **repeat attention already authorized for deterministic reuse**, which should be removed;
3. **semantic identity misses**, which remain explicit cost;
4. **false Probe nominations**, which remain governed but measurable burden;
5. **benchmark-control artifacts**, which must not be exposed to the runtime operator.

No dollar claim should be based on eliminating required human authority.

## 17. Provider-cost analysis

T0's provider gap is $0.021422. The clearest post-hoc reuse opportunity is only about $0.0071. Wide coupling may increase prompt cost. Provider crossover therefore requires either a new T1 workload containing more valid deterministic reuse, cheaper sparse routing without added calls, or lower prompt/output burden while retaining sufficiency. T1 must report provider dollars separately from attention and harm composites.

## 18. Logical-call analysis

The T0 call gap is plausibly removable in count: 60 coupled calls are theoretically consolidated into 30 wide calls, taking 205 to 145. A further six I1 repeats may be bypassed. This is the strongest opportunity signal in T0.

Call count is not the optimization objective. A wide call may use more tokens and cost more. T1 must report logical calls, attempts, tokens, cost, latency, and provider failures independently.

## 19. Strongest counterarguments

1. **The proposed optimization attacks the wrong cost.** Human attention, not provider work, dominates T0 composite loss.
2. **Wide calls may cost more.** T0 already suggests this for coupled decisions.
3. **Mode-selection metadata may be incomplete.** Missing coupling or invalidation declarations could produce unsafe under-projection.
4. **Reuse can create stale suppression.** Exact identity is safe only while every material validity condition remains bound.
5. **A controller can become hidden policy.** If thresholds decide what is material or whether a Gate applies, it is architecture/policy, not implementation hardening.
6. **T0 workload is contaminated and synthetic.** Category opportunities may not reproduce in a clean T1 corpus.
7. **More modes increase operational complexity.** Routing bugs, telemetry gaps, and provider variance may erase theoretical gains.

## 20. Failure modes and deterministic fail-closed behavior

| Failure | Required fail-closed response |
|---|---|
| Reuse identity or validity uncertain | Do not suppress; use existing examination path |
| Gate `required_evidence` incomplete or stale | Do not call Resolver; surface evidence/Gate deficiency |
| Coupling not declared | Preserve current sparse/full examination behavior; no heuristic widening |
| Conflict, invalidation, lifecycle, or authority change present | Force full governed examination |
| Wide surface includes evidence outside Gate reachability | Reject Payload construction |
| Model requests more evidence or authority | Reject request; model cannot expand aperture |

## 21. Architecture-gap assessment

No conceptual architecture gap is demonstrated at proposal time. Existing contracts appear sufficient for deterministic reuse, bounded projection, Gate-declared joint projection, and full examination.

Potential Domain Pack work is limited to accurate existing declarations of `required_evidence`, coupling/decomposability, effect reachability, and invalidators. If a pack does not express those semantics, v1 must not invent them dynamically. A proposed new declaration after corpus observation would require separate Domain Pack review.

Return `ADAPTIVE APERTURE REQUIRES ARCHITECTURE REVIEW` if implementation requires a persisted aperture primitive, new authority, model-controlled bypass, Probe expansion, or ungoverned state exposure.

## 22. Proposed T1 falsification design

### Objective

Determine on a new sealed corpus whether Adaptive Resolver Aperture reduces system burden without reducing correctness, introducing critical harm, weakening authority, or hiding semantic uncertainty.

### Arms

- **H0:** frozen current HELM execution strategy at the T1 implementation-parent commit.
- **H1:** same HELM plus Adaptive Resolver Aperture v1, with identical authority and Domain Pack.
- **B:** NND-authored competent broad-context baseline using the same provider/model/configuration.

### New corpus

Recommended starting design: 240 paired tasks, 40 per preregistered stratum:

1. valid deterministic reuse and already-satisfied conditions;
2. sparse/decomposable consequential surfaces;
3. coupled jointly sufficient surfaces, including 3, 10, 25, 50, and 100 facts;
4. conflict, invalidation, lifecycle, and authority-sensitive full examinations;
5. structurally unmodeled Probe/discovery cases including false nominations;
6. steering-boundary adversaries: incomplete coupling declarations, stale lineage, near-match identity, hidden invalidator, misleading cost advantage, and ambiguous mode eligibility.

Task/category labels, expected mode, ground truth, harm taxonomy, and authority schedule remain sealed. Corpus composition and thresholds freeze before implementation evaluation. T0 tasks, IDs, wording, and control schedules are prohibited.

### Hidden benchmark-authority interface

To exercise the full pipeline without exposing a control schedule, an independent NND/scorer-owned service or staged sealed process should return only the next authorized benchmark-human event after receiving a hash-bound Gate/candidate request. BHE must not see future controls or ground truth. The interface must distinguish benchmark authority from real production human authority and is valid only inside the synthetic experiment.

### Freeze sequence

1. NND freezes design, corpus generator, corpus, ground truth, scoring, authority schedule, baseline prompt, and retry policy.
2. Public runtime corpus and adapter contract are hash-committed without sealed labels/controls.
3. BHE freezes H0, H1, B configurations and the candidate code commit.
4. Both HELM arms and B execute paired tasks with checkpointed attempts.
5. Outputs and telemetry are hash-committed before unsealing.
6. Independent scorer applies frozen rules and publishes machine-readable evidence.

### Measurements

Per task and mode: selected execution path and deterministic basis; active evidence IDs/bytes/tokens; calls and attempts; tokens; cost; latency; human examinations; Probe events; suppressions; invalidations; Gate/Governor events; correctness; harms; hidden identity misses; unnecessary widening; and provider failures.

### Statistical plan for NND freeze

- Safety gates run first and are absolute for authority violations, stale suppressions, improper bypass, hidden identity misses, model-selected authority, ungoverned exposure, and invalidation bypass.
- Correctness: paired H1 versus H0 and H1 versus sealed ground truth. Recommend a one-sided non-inferiority margin no larger than 1.25 percentage points, frozen by NND, plus exact paired tests and confidence intervals. H1 must not trade correctness for cost.
- Economics: paired H1-H0 and H1-B comparisons on provider cost, attention, and frozen composite. Use a preregistered paired nonparametric test, effect estimate, bootstrap confidence interval, and both statistical and material thresholds.
- Mode diagnostics are secondary and cannot override pooled safety/correctness.

## 29. Recommendation

**PROCEED TO T1 DESIGN FREEZE.**

The candidate is expressible as existing-contract enforcement and implementation hardening, subject to NND confirming the hidden benchmark-authority interface and the fail-closed steering table. The proposal should proceed because T0 identifies a real logical-call topology problem and because a clean T1 can falsify it cheaply relative to implementation rollout.

The recommendation is not a prediction of economic success. T0 presently suggests that call-count savings may not translate into provider or composite crossover, and that required human authority may define the dominant operating boundary. NND should reject or revise the proposal if it concludes that the controller would become hidden policy, that the T1 authority interface is not scientifically valid, or that T0's removable burden is too small to justify implementation.
